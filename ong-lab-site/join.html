Postdoctoral fellow, VA Boston

We are seeking a PhD-trained postdoctoral fellow to lead analyses of national VA Corporate Data Warehouse data with our VA collaborators. Now recruiting

Research assistant (full-time), Boston Medical Center

We are recruiting a full-time research assistant with strong computational and statistical skills. The lab focuses on risk stratification, prognostication and digital biomarkers in critically ill neurological patients, with an emphasis on rigorous quantitative methods and clinically meaningful research.

You will review, clean and analyze quantitative pupillometry data and heterogeneous clinical data, including brain imaging, EEG and EOG waveforms, and structured and unstructured data from the electronic health record. Current projects include quantitative pupillometry and neurologic deterioration in a Neuro ICU cohort of more than 2,500 patients, eye movements measured by electrooculography in comatose patients after cardiac arrest, and multicenter analyses of prognosis after large ischemic stroke. The role also includes some clinical data collection and abstraction, IRB and regulatory work, and helping prepare figures, presentations and manuscripts, with strong mentorship and opportunities for authorship.

What we are looking for

  • Strong proficiency in R and Python, with clean, reproducible, version-controlled code (Git)
  • Statistical modeling, including regression, survival analysis, model validation and handling of missing data
  • Experience training and evaluating machine learning and deep learning models, such as gradient boosting, Transformers and LSTMs
  • Comfort turning clinical questions into analysis plans and communicating results clearly to clinicians and statisticians

Preferred: a background in biostatistics, statistics, data science or computer science; contributions to peer-reviewed research or open-source projects; interest in large language models; and interest in a PhD or longer-term research career.

To apply or learn more, email Dr. Ong at cjong@bu.edu.

Who we work with

  • BU Chobanian & Avedisian medical students, from first to fourth year
  • Masters students in Medical Sciences (MAMS), Biostatistics and Public Health
  • Undergraduates and post-baccalaureate research assistants
  • Engineers, data scientists and visiting collaborators

What you will gain

Our trainees have led first-author papers, given oral presentations at the American Academy of Neurology, American Neurological Association and Neurocritical Care Society meetings, and matched into competitive residencies. You will learn to work with clinical data warehouse data, physiologic signals, imaging and clinical notes, and to apply statistics and machine learning to questions that matter at the bedside.

How to apply

Email Dr. Ong at cjong@bu.edu with your CV, your program and year, the projects or topics that interest you, and the hours you can commit each week. Prior coding experience is helpful but not required.
