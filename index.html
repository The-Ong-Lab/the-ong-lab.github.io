Data-driven neurocritical care

In the neuro ICU, the brain can change within hours. We build tools that answer the two questions families and clinicians ask most: who is about to get worse, and who is likely to recover.

We combine continuous bedside sensors, imaging, clinical notes and machine learning to detect neurologic decline before it is clinically apparent and to personalize predictions of recovery after stroke, hemorrhage, traumatic brain injury and cardiac arrest.

What we do

From bedside signal to clinical decision

Monitor

Quantitative pupillometry, non-invasive intracranial pressure waveforms, eye tracking, electrooculography and wearable sensors that capture brain physiology continuously instead of at periodic bedside checks.

Monitoring tools

Detect decline

Models that flag cerebral edema and neurologic deterioration after stroke, hemorrhage and traumatic brain injury hours before they are clinically or radiographically apparent.

Detecting deterioration

Predict recovery

Quantitative eye movements, EEG and multimodal models that identify which patients after cardiac arrest and in disorders of consciousness are most likely to recover.

Predicting recovery

Why Boston Medical Center

Built where the need is greatest

BMC is the largest safety-net hospital in New England, caring for a high-acuity and highly diverse population. That makes it an ideal place to develop AI that generalizes to real-world care, rather than to a narrow academic cohort.
